The 12 Houses

What each house governs — body, life, and karma

In Vedic astrology, the birth chart is divided into twelve houses (bhavas). Each house governs a specific set of life areas. The sign on a house, the planets sitting in it, and the strength of the planet that rules it together describe how that area of life unfolds. This guide explains each house in plain language — no prior knowledge assumed.

1st House — Lagna (The Self)

The first house is the ascendant, or Lagna — the sign rising on the eastern horizon at the moment of birth. It represents the self: your body, your physical constitution, your temperament, your overall vitality, and the lens through which the whole chart is read.

A strong first house gives robust health, a clear sense of identity, and natural confidence. The planet that rules the first house (the Lagna lord) is one of the most important planets in the entire chart — its placement colours your whole life direction.

Body parts: head, brain, overall constitution. Karaka (natural significator): the Sun.

2nd House — Dhana (Wealth & Speech)

The second house governs accumulated wealth, savings, family lineage, food, and speech. It is not income earned (that is the eleventh) but what you hold and keep — your bank balance, possessions, and the family you were born into.

It also rules the face, mouth, and voice — so it speaks to communication, honesty, and the way you express yourself verbally.

Body parts: face, mouth, teeth, throat. Karaka: Jupiter (wealth) and Mercury (speech).

3rd House — Sahaja (Courage & Effort)

The third house represents courage, initiative, younger siblings, short journeys, hobbies, and the hands. It is the house of self-effort — the willingness to take action and push through resistance.

Strong third house people are enterprising, brave, and skilled with their hands or in communication. It also governs writing, media, and personal skill.

Body parts: arms, hands, shoulders, ears. Karaka: Mars.

4th House — Sukha (Home & Heart)

The fourth house governs the mother, home, land, property, vehicles, emotional security, and inner peace (sukha means happiness). It is the foundation of the chart — the roots from which everything else grows.

A strong fourth house gives emotional contentment, a stable home, and good relationships with the mother. It also rules education in its early and formative sense.

Body parts: chest, heart, lungs. Karaka: the Moon (mother, mind) and Venus (vehicles, comforts).

5th House — Putra (Creativity & Children)

The fifth house represents intelligence, creativity, children, romance, speculation, and past-life merit (purva punya). It is the house of what you create — whether children, art, or ideas.

It governs the capacity for joy, play, and genuine self-expression. It is also the house of mantras and devotion. A strong fifth house gives sharp intelligence and creative fertility.

Body parts: upper abdomen, stomach. Karaka: Jupiter.

6th House — Ari (Obstacles & Service)

The sixth house governs enemies, debts, disease, daily work, service, and obstacles. Though considered a difficult (dusthana) house, it is also the house of overcoming — the discipline to defeat what stands against you.

It rules the capacity to work hard, to serve, to recover from illness, and to win in competition and litigation. Strong sixth-house people are resilient and good at handling adversity.

Body parts: lower abdomen, intestines, kidneys. Karaka: Mars and Saturn.

7th House — Yuvati (Partnership)

The seventh house governs marriage, the spouse, business partnerships, and all one-to-one relationships. It is directly opposite the first house — the 'other' to your 'self'.

It also represents public dealings, contracts, and the general public. The condition of the seventh house and its lord describes the nature and quality of your closest partnerships.

Body parts: lower back, reproductive organs. Karaka: Venus.

8th House — Randhra (Transformation)

The eighth house governs longevity, sudden events, inheritance, joint resources, the occult, and deep transformation. It is the most mysterious house — the place of endings that become beginnings.

It rules research, hidden things, and anything that requires going beneath the surface. Difficult when afflicted, but the source of profound depth and resilience when supported. It is also the house of the partner's wealth.

Body parts: reproductive and excretory organs. Karaka: Saturn.

9th House — Dharma (Fortune & Faith)

The ninth house is one of the most auspicious houses, governing fortune, higher wisdom, the father, gurus, long journeys, pilgrimage, and dharma (life purpose and ethics).

It represents grace — the good luck that seems to arrive unearned, which classical astrology attributes to past-life merit. A strong ninth house gives faith, a good teacher, and a fortunate life direction.

Body parts: hips, thighs. Karaka: Jupiter and the Sun (father).

10th House — Karma (Career & Status)

The tenth house governs career, profession, social status, authority, and public reputation. It sits at the highest point of the chart — what the world sees you achieve.

It represents your action in the world (karma in the sense of work) and the recognition that follows. A strong tenth house gives a successful, visible career and respected standing.

Body parts: knees, joints, bones. Karaka: the Sun, Mercury, Jupiter, and Saturn all signify career.

11th House — Labha (Gains & Networks)

The eleventh house governs income, gains, profits, friends, networks, elder siblings, and the fulfilment of desires. Whatever the tenth house earns through work, the eleventh house is where it lands as actual gain.

It rules large groups, communities, and the achievement of goals. A strong eleventh house gives steady income, helpful friends, and the realisation of ambitions.

Body parts: calves, ankles. Karaka: Jupiter.

12th House — Vyaya (Loss & Liberation)

The twelfth house governs loss, expenditure, foreign lands, isolation, sleep, and ultimately spiritual liberation (moksha). It is the house of letting go — of release, withdrawal, and surrender.

Though associated with loss, it is also the house of the bed (rest and intimacy), of charity, of life abroad, and of the deepest spiritual practice. What the twelfth house 'loses' to the material world it gains for the soul.

Body parts: feet, left eye. Karaka: Saturn (loss) and Ketu (liberation).

How to Read the Houses Together

No house works alone. The 'house lord' — the planet ruling the sign on that house — carries the house's energy to wherever it sits. For example, if the tenth lord (career) sits in the eleventh house (gains), career naturally produces income.

Houses also fall into natural groups: the kendras (1, 4, 7, 10) are the pillars of the chart; the trikonas (1, 5, 9) are the houses of fortune and dharma; the dusthanas (6, 8, 12) are the houses of difficulty and growth. A planet's value depends heavily on which group it occupies.

When reading any area of life, look at three things together: the house itself, the planets in it, and the strength and placement of its lord. The agreement or disagreement between these three tells the real story.
